Scholarship application checklist: documents, timeline and common mistakes

A practical, month-by-month checklist for Indian students applying to government and private scholarships.

By the iwin.in editorial team. Updated 16 October 2026.

Most scholarship applications fail for avoidable reasons: a missing certificate, a name that doesn't match across documents, or a submission on the last evening when the portal slows down. This checklist helps you get the basics right so your merit is what gets judged.

Eight weeks before: build your document kit

  • Identity: Aadhaar, with your name spelled exactly as on your marksheets.
  • Bank account in the student's own name, Aadhaar-seeded and active. Many government scholarships pay by direct benefit transfer and fail if the account isn't linked.
  • Income certificate issued by the competent authority for the current financial year.
  • Category certificate (SC, ST, OBC-NCL, EWS) if applicable, and a disability certificate if applicable.
  • Domicile certificate for state schemes.
  • Academic records: the previous qualifying exam's marksheet, plus current admission and fee receipts.
  • Bonafide certificate from your institution.
  • Photograph and signature scanned to the size limits the portal states.

Scan each document as a clear PDF or JPEG, name files plainly (for example income-certificate-2026.pdf) and keep them in one folder.

Six weeks before: check eligibility line by line

Open the official notice and copy every eligibility rule into a list: income ceiling, minimum percentage, course type, year of study, age, state and whether you can hold two scholarships at once. Tick each one against your documents. If any line is unclear, ask your institution's scholarship cell now, not on deadline day.

Four weeks before: register and draft

  • Register on the portal and save your login details somewhere safe. On the National Scholarship Portal, students complete a one-time registration before applying.
  • Draft any essays or statements of purpose. Read our essay guide.
  • Request reference letters and give referees at least three weeks.

Two weeks before: submit

Submit early. Portals get slow in the final days, and late submissions leave no time to fix errors. After submitting, download or screenshot the acknowledgement with your application number.

After you submit

  • Many government scholarships need verification by your institution and then by a district or state authority. Follow up with your institution so your application doesn't stall at the first level.
  • Check your application status regularly and respond quickly to any request for corrections.
  • Keep the account active; dormant accounts can block payments.

Common mistakes

MistakeHow to avoid it
Name mismatch across Aadhaar, bank and marksheetCorrect records before applying, or attach an affidavit if the scheme allows
Old income certificateGet one for the current financial year
Wrong scheme selectedMatch the scheme to your course level and category carefully
Applying on the last dayAim to submit two weeks early
Ignoring renewalRenewals have their own deadlines; put them in your calendar
Add every deadline to the deadline planner and export a calendar reminder three days before.

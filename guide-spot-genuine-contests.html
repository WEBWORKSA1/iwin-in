How to spot genuine contests and avoid scams in India

The seven warning signs of fake contests, scholarships and prize messages, and the checks that take two minutes.

By the iwin.in editorial team. Updated 16 October 2026.

Free contests and scholarships are real, and so are the scams that copy them. Fraudsters know students are hopeful and short on time, and they borrow the language of genuine programmes: "selected", "congratulations", "limited seats". The good news is that scams follow a small number of patterns. Learn them once and you'll recognise almost every fake you receive.

The one rule that catches most scams

Genuine organisers do not charge you to receive a prize or a scholarship. If a message asks for a "processing fee", "GST on the prize", "courier charge" or "registration amount" before you can claim something, stop. This single test filters out most fraud we see reported.

Seven warning signs

  1. You won something you never entered. Real contests can only pick winners from entries. A surprise win is a hook.
  2. They want an OTP, UPI PIN or card details. No organiser needs these to give you money. An OTP authorises a payment from you.
  3. The sender isn't official. A government scheme writing from a personal Gmail address or a random WhatsApp number is a red flag. Check that links go to the organiser's real domain, such as gov.in or nic.in for government portals.
  4. Extreme urgency. "Claim within 30 minutes" is designed to stop you checking. Genuine programmes give days or weeks.
  5. No official rules. Legitimate contests publish eligibility, how winners are chosen and the prize. If you can't find rules, assume there are none.
  6. Look-alike websites. One extra letter, a hyphen or an unusual ending in the address can mean a copied site. Type the address yourself rather than tapping a link.
  7. Remote-access apps. Anyone asking you to install a screen-sharing or "verification" app wants control of your phone and bank apps.

A two-minute verification routine

  1. Search for the programme's name and go to the official site from the search results, not from the message.
  2. Find the official rules or notice and check the dates, eligibility and how results are communicated.
  3. Compare the sender's address or number with the contact details on the official site.
  4. If you're still unsure, ask a teacher, your college's scholarship cell or the organiser through its official contact page.

Scholarship-specific traps

Fake scholarship sites often promise "guaranteed" awards or charge to "apply on your behalf". Applying for government scholarships on the National Scholarship Portal is free, and you can do it yourself. Paid agents add risk because they handle your Aadhaar, bank and income documents.

Be careful with forms that ask for scans of every document before you've seen any official notice. Share documents only on the official portal or with your institution's verifying officer.

If you've already paid or shared details

Act fast. Call your bank to block the card or account, then report at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the 1930 helpline. Quick reporting gives banks a chance to hold the transfer. Keep screenshots, phone numbers, UPI IDs and transaction references.
